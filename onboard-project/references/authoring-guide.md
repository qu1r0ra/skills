# Project onboarding and learning resources

## Read by task

For resource authoring, read Use case, Precedence, Rules, Humanizer, and
Deviation rule. Then consult only the relevant Techniques:

- Audience depth and prerequisites: A and E; the Embedded Rust Book exemplar.
- Retention or transfer: B.

Read Provenance and Coverage for guide maintenance or evidence review. The

## Use case

Author a self-contained resource that onboards a particular group to a project.
Projects may involve code or other work; the audience may be subject beginners
or experts who are new to the project. The mission is the owner's reason for
onboarding them. Outcomes specify what they should explain, evaluate, or do.
The owner confirms scope, prior knowledge, constraints, preferences, medium,
and what self-containment means for this package before authoring.

Venue: none. Quality means traceable project facts, depth and challenge suited
to the audience, and a usable package for the agreed purpose. A new project run
establishes its own venue, institutional rules, access needs, and rights basis
for any borrowed material. The sources below establish authoring guidance,
not a universal submission structure, legal regime, or output format.

## Precedence

Apply `requirement` > `authoritative guideline` > `empirical` > `convention`
> `preference`. Higher grades win conflicts within their stated scope.
An institution's course guidance remains guidance when adapted to a resource.
Owner decisions define the mission and delivery; they do not increase a rule's
evidence grade.

## Rules

### Requirement

No external requirement is established for this venue-free use case. Check
the actual project's applicable rules before treating a particular standard,
format, licence, or submission limit as mandatory.

### Authoritative guideline

1. **Audience needs.** Select content from the gap between the knowledge or
   skills the audience needs and what it already has. Google's advice concerns
   technical documentation; apply this audience analysis to the agreed project
   purpose, with relevant knowledge as well as tasks in view.
   Source: [Google, Audience](https://developers.google.com/tech-writing/one/audience):
   "knowledge and skills your audience needs to do a task".

2. **Prior knowledge.** Establish relevant prior knowledge and connect it to
   new material. Subject expertise and project familiarity are separate inputs
   in this workflow (an owner-confirmed adaptation); classroom diagnostics are
   not required for every resource.
   Source: [Cornell, Prior knowledge](https://teaching.cornell.edu/teaching-resources/assessment-evaluation/assessing-prior-knowledge-addressing-learning-gaps):
   "create a bridge between students’ previous knowledge and new material".

3. **Observable outcomes.** Resolve an aim such as understanding into what a
   reader could explain, evaluate, or do. Cornell's course-design guidance
   supports observable outcomes; a resource need not administer a formal exam.
   Source: [Cornell, Learning outcomes](https://teaching.cornell.edu/setting-learning-outcomes):
   "Avoid verbs that are unclear and cannot be observed and measured easily".

4. **Outcome fit.** Choose activities to match intended outcomes and their
   expected evidence. Applying collaborative-course guidance to an explanation,
   critique prompt, or individual project exercise is an explicit adaptation.
   The owner-confirmed outcome decides whether practice is needed.
   Source: [Cornell, Groundwork](https://teaching.cornell.edu/teaching-resources/active-collaborative-learning/collaborative-learning/laying-groundwork):
   "the activities you design for your students should closely align with your learning outcomes or assessment expectations".

5. **Useful terminology.** Explain useful but unfamiliar jargon on first use
   or link to a trusted definition. Familiar field terms can remain precise;
   project-specific meanings still need context. This is developer-documentation
   guidance adapted to the agreed audience.
   Source: [Google, Jargon](https://developers.google.com/style/jargon):
   "briefly describe the term in parentheses on first reference, or link to a trusted definition".

6. **Descriptive headings.** Use headings that help readers locate the subject
   they need. Google's page-navigation guidance directly fits written digital
   resources; adapt it to the navigation available in the chosen medium.
   Source: [Google, Headings](https://developers.google.com/style/headings):
   "Use descriptive headings and titles because they help a reader navigate their browser and the page".

### Preference

The owner confirmed these workflow choices on 2026-10-18. They are preferences,
not findings about all learners:

7. **Mission and brief.** Author for the owner-confirmed project brief. Keep
   project-specific decisions available for future revisions. The skill's
   framing steps own the interview and confirmation procedure.

8. **Evidence.** Ground project explanations in authoritative project files
   and primary sources. Resolve material gaps after inspection and confirmation;
   distinguish the implementation, intended design, and unresolved proposals.
   Keep essential claims traceable and state material uncertainty.

9. **Package.** Choose the format for this mission. Supply the prerequisites,
   entry point, explanations, and assets needed within the agreed standalone
   boundary. External sources can extend that package. HTML, quizzes, custom
   visuals, optional answers, and print or offline delivery are per-run choices.

10. **Evaluation boundary.** Check the artifact's correctness, audience fit,
    outcome coverage, and promised behavior. Report what was actually checked;
    claims of learner retention or transfer need learner evidence. This workflow
    produces resources; live teaching and individual learner history remain
    with the separately user-invoked `teach` workflow.


## Techniques

These are conditional options for the confirmed outcomes. The empirical grade
belongs to the findings; the onboarding applications are scoped inferences,
not measured effects for this workflow.

### Empirical

A. **Support depends on prior knowledge.** Spanjers et al. (2011) randomized
   76 Dutch secondary students to segmented or continuous animated probability
   examples; one was excluded for missing data, leaving 75 in the analysis.
   Segmentation improved learning efficiency for lower-prior-knowledge
   students, not higher-prior-knowledge students. Consider an introductory route
   with a way to skip familiar material; the study concerns one subject, age
   group, and medium, not professional project orientation.
   Source: [Spanjers et al., original article](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/72615174/spanjers_2011_an_expertise_reversal_effect.pdf):
   "only for students with lower levels of prior knowledge".

B. **Retrieval when it fits.** Butler (2010) compared repeated testing and
   restudy of prose passages in four experiments with undergraduate psychology
   students. A final test one week later included novel inference questions;
   repeated testing improved retention and transfer relative to restudy.
   Initial tests included correct-answer feedback, whose contribution was not
   isolated. Consider an explanation or application prompt with feedback when
   retention or transfer matters; this evidence does not mandate quizzes in
   every resource or establish mastery for this audience.
   Source: [Butler, original article](https://andymatuschak.org/files/papers/Butler%20-%202010%20-%20Repeated%20Testing%20Produces%20Superior%20Transfer%20of%20Learning%20Relative%20to%20Repeated.pdf):
   "Repeated testing produced superior retention and transfer".

C. **Worked examples for suitable tasks.** Barbieri et al. (2023) synthesized
   55 experimental or quasi-experimental mathematics studies with 181 effect
   sizes. The average effect on mathematics performance was medium (g = 0.48).
   Prior knowledge was measured inconsistently. Consider a worked example for
   a procedural outcome; this result does not establish the best sequence for
   every domain or expert audience.
   Source: [Barbieri et al., original article](https://www.danamillercotto.com/uploads/4/7/7/2/47725475/barbieri_et_al__2023__we_meta-analysis.pdf):
   "the average effect size of worked examples on mathematics performance outcomes was medium".

D. **Visuals need an explanatory purpose.** Berney and Betrancourt (2016)
   synthesized 61 studies (7,036 participants, 140 comparisons) of animation
   against static graphics. The average advantage was small (g = 0.226), with
   high heterogeneity. Select a visual to explain a relationship or process;
   animation is a choice to justify for this content and audience.
   Source: [Berney and Betrancourt, original article](https://access.archive-ouverte.unige.ch/access/metadata/75bf6876-cb91-4e2c-8ac6-9d0767449449/download):
   "the overall ES has a small magnitude".

E. **Experts can be project newcomers.** Yates (2014) used Grounded Theory
   with 12 recorded onboarding sessions across eight organizations, plus
   interviews and questionnaires. This qualitative software study distinguishes
   skilled developers from people familiar with a particular codebase. Consider
   orienting experts to project representations, information routes, and work
   practices. It compares no instructional treatment and supplies no measured
   effectiveness claim for non-code projects.
   Source: [Yates, original thesis](https://researchrepository.ul.ie/server/api/core/bitstreams/7179c081-688e-4aca-8d28-a11af442f10a/content):
   "Newcomers are skilled software developers who lack specific knowledge of the code they will work on".

### Convention

Exemplars are link-and-technique notes, checked 2026-10-18. No full text or
assets are captured. Their licences apply to their material, not these notes.

- [The Embedded Rust Book](https://doc.rust-lang.org/stable/embedded-book/)
  identifies readers with different starting expertise, lists assumptions,
  and provides catch-up routes. This illustrates separating kinds of prior
  knowledge. Prose: CC BY-SA 4.0; code: MIT or Apache 2.0, per the
  [repository licence statement](https://github.com/rust-embedded/book).
  Source: Rust Embedded Working Group, Who This Book is For:
  "either some embedded background or some Rust background".
- [The Unix Shell](https://swcarpentry.github.io/shell-novice/instructor/index.html)
  states prerequisites, a progressive schedule, and episode questions and
  objectives. Borrow the technique of making readiness and intended outcomes
  visible; its novice pacing is specific to that lesson. Instructional material
  is CC BY 4.0 per the [lesson licence](https://github.com/swcarpentry/shell-novice/blob/main/LICENSE.md).
  Source: The Carpentries, prerequisites:
  "If you have stored files on a computer at all".
- [The Python Tutorial](https://docs.python.org/3/tutorial/index.html) bounds
  its scope and provides standalone examples with deeper references. This
  illustrates self-containment within an explicit boundary, not a requirement
  to include every related subject. Documentation: PSF License Version 2;

  Source: Python Software Foundation, tutorial introduction:
  "all examples are self-contained, so the tutorial can be read off-line as well".

## Humanizer

Humanizer is on when activated by the owning repository's instructions or an
explicit request. In AIOS, root `AGENTS.md` permits activation only through
`follow-writing-guide` or a direct request. This guide does not invoke it.
Once active, apply it to explanatory prose after drafting, preserving technical
terms, facts, citations, metadata, and source quotations.

Collisions: rule 5's necessary terminology takes precedence over §12 (Overused
AI words) when the word has a technical meaning. Rule 6's descriptive headings
take precedence over §20 (Decorative headings) if a proposed rewrite loses the
navigation meaning; sentence case is compatible. Rule 3's explicit outcomes
remain necessary even when §25 (Writing about the document instead of its
subject) would remove genuine reader orientation. These are scoped conflicts,
not permission for decorative or repetitive prose. Preference rules yield to
Humanizer in prose style while their confirmed workflow decisions remain intact.
Humanizer never changes an authoritative rule's wording or effect, or rewrites
a quoted passage to remove a tell.

## Deviation rule

The author may deviate from a `convention` or `preference` with a one-line reason
in the draft. A deviation from a `requirement` or `authoritative guideline` needs
the author's explicit approval for that passage. Resolve new user-owned choices
in the onboarding brief; preserve evidence grades when recording an exception.
